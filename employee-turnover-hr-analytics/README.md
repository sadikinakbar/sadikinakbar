# 📈 Employee Turnover & HR Analytics

> **Turning employee data into practical HR insights for workforce and retention decisions.**

---

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Employee turnover is not simply a number. It can be an important signal of workforce stability, employee experience, operational conditions, and management effectiveness.

This portfolio presents a practical HR analytics framework for understanding:

- Employee turnover
- Resignation patterns
- Workforce trends
- Potential retention risks
- Employee movement
- HR intervention priorities

The objective is to transform workforce information into **practical insights that support better HR and management decisions.**

---

## 💼 Business Context

High or unexpected employee turnover can affect:

- Workforce continuity
- Recruitment workload
- Operational productivity
- Training and onboarding costs
- Employee engagement
- Knowledge retention

For operationally intensive businesses such as **Mining, Heavy Equipment, Hospitality, Logistics, Manufacturing, and Site Operations**, understanding workforce movement is particularly important.

HR analytics can help management move from:

> **"How many employees resigned?"**

to:

> **"What patterns can we identify, and what actions should HR take?"**

---

## 🎯 Objective

This framework is designed to help HR teams:

1. Monitor employee turnover trends
2. Identify resignation patterns
3. Understand workforce movement
4. Identify potential retention risks
5. Support HR intervention planning
6. Improve workforce stability
7. Provide structured insights for management decisions

## 📊 HR Analytics Framework

### 01 — Turnover Monitoring

Monitor employee movement over a defined period.

**Key indicators:**

- Total headcount
- New hires
- Resignations
- Employee exits
- Internal movement
- Monthly turnover trend

A basic turnover indicator can be expressed as:

**Turnover Rate = Employee Exits ÷ Average Headcount × 100%**

The metric should be reviewed together with workforce context rather than treated as a standalone number.

---

### 02 — Resignation Pattern Analysis

Analyze resignation patterns to identify areas that may require further investigation.

Possible dimensions include:

- Department
- Position
- Length of service
- Location
- Employment type
- Period or month
- Operational function

The purpose is not simply to identify who left, but to understand **where workforce stability may require attention.**
